1. Business / product overview

CarGurus' consumer experience is a search-and-filter funnel: SRP, VDP, lead form. Generative AI made a different shape possible, where a shopper describes what they want and something does the work. By early 2026 several teams were building toward that at once (Discover, Universal AI Mode, Agentic Outreach, AutoFetch, Dealership Mode), each with its own roadmap and its own idea of what "Guru" meant.

In July the org consolidated these under Guru as the H2 umbrella, split into co-pilot (AI helps you shop) and auto-pilot (AI does the work for you). Raph Wallheimer became PM for both Guru Co-Pilot and Guru Auto-Pilot Search. (H2 Gameplan)

Quarterly planning is how that umbrella reached squads. Q3 guidance organized teams under co-pilot and auto-pilot pillars. Q4 guidance reorganized them around a 3x3 "menu of consumer experiences": Shop, Connect, and Visit, each at three levels of Guru involvement (Self-Driver, Co-Pilot, Concierge). (Q3 guidance, Q4 guidance)

2. Problem / purpose

Two problems, and the second is the real one.

Stated: consumers don't want to become car experts, and the funnel assumes they will. Meanwhile Google showed at I/O 2026 that it can chain agents into a full car-shopping journey where CarGurus supplies the inventory and none of the experience. (personas doc)

Actual: the company had a long-term vision and quarterly delivery teams, and nothing in between. Ideas were either too speculative to schedule or already committed to a sprint. And the exec layer said so directly: in August, Andrew relayed ET feedback that the strategy was "theoretical and hard to picture, bring the vision to life for us." (Slack)

H2 is the name for that middle layer: near-term, not next-sprint. Concrete enough to test, early enough to change direction.

3. Users and audience

Consumers, framed by how much they want to delegate rather than by demographic. Auto-pilot shoppers are upper funnel, still forming a consideration set, time-constrained or low-confidence, and likely mobile. Co-pilot shoppers are lower funnel with roughly two vehicles in mind and want sharper comparison and validation tools. (personas doc)

Internal stakeholders, who were the immediate audience for almost every artifact. The PM needed a strawman to plan a half around. Executives needed to see the end state to fund it. Delivery teams (PRSN, S&D) needed something concrete enough to build from. Research needed stimulus.

5. Scope and constraints

  • No team, no sprint, no budget. Horizon work had to earn its place by being more convincing than the roadmap.
  • Two designers, overlapping concepts. Calla's My Guru work and Chris's Hub work covered similar ground with no agreed owner in July.
  • Naming. UAI and Discover converging on a single "Ask Guru" brand with no agreed separation, flagged as blocking entry-point placement. Later, "Text the Guru" was renamed "Text Guru" over cultural appropriation concerns. (Sep 25 email)
  • Infrastructure the design assumed. Hidden listings existed only on the mobile app, so "Not interested" had to be scoped to Hub-only for v1. Anything past MVP depended on an orchestration layer (now Guru Core) that didn't exist yet.
  • Gated sequencing. Guru Hub Phase 1 can't launch until notifications win, and Notifications V1 and V2 were both stat-sig neutral.
  • Platform limits as a feature. The RCS prototype had no UI to hide behind.

6. Process

  1. Proposed the role before it existed, then found the PM who needed it.
  2. Mapped consumers by degree of delegation, and wrote down the competitive threat in a single sentence.
  3. Wireframed the end state so the PM had a strawman to plan the half around.
  4. Split everything into Q3 now, Q4 to Q1 next, and H2 future, and said which was which out loud.
  5. Handed artifacts to research so opinion became data, and let the data overrule me where it did.
  6. Turned the strategy team's framework into nine concrete cells and carried it through Q4 planning, so every squad could see where its work sat.
  7. Built a working prototype when static frames stopped persuading, and let delivery teams take the surfaces over.
  8. Handed the two biggest threads off to their own teams, and to their own case studies.
