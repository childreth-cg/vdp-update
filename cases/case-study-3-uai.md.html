Case Study 3: Universal AI (UAI), from January framing to contextual entry points

Status: working draft, updated weekly Last updated: 2026-11-02

Working titles

  • The FAB we shipped, and the entry points that replaced it
  • Designing conversation before anyone agreed what it could do
  • Putting AI where the job is

1. Business / product overview

In December 2025 the Consumer AI committee set two parallel tracks: make Discover a self-contained AI shopping product, and add a "universal AI mode" to the SRP and VDP (Q1 Agentic SO Critical Path, Consumer AI Committee). The committee targeted full production by end of Q2 with lead conversion as the do-no-harm metric.

UAI became the page-embedded side of that bet. It lives inside SRP and VDP, acts on the page (applies filters, submits leads), and carries one conversation across both (Guru Core: How UAI Will Work).

2. Problem / purpose

January: nobody had agreed what the thing did. Product was asking what designs looked like, engineering was asking what to scope, and design was asking what the scope was. Alyssa's read on 1/15 was that each group was waiting on the other (Consumer AI Pod 1/15). The open questions were whether it was a smart Q&A or multi-turn, whether it filtered results, and whether context survived SRP to VDP.

After launch: reach. In the V.1 MVT (Jul 8 to Aug 9), under 0.5% of eligible users opened UAI: 0.40% mobile, 0.23% desktop. Openers converted well on VDP, but mobile VDP-to-lead dropped 0.79% stat sig and Discover mobile lead users fell 66% after its banner was pulled (PDA UAI Analyses). Mobile full-screen takeover meant shoppers couldn't see filters apply; 35.67% of mobile openers closed without interacting (CNAI-445).

The through-line is entry and layout. The contextual vs consistent entry point question I raised in January is the question the Q3 work reopened.

3. Users and audience

Shoppers on SRP and VDP, mobile first.

Internal, which in January was the real audience: PMs Sydney Bigelow and Caitlin Endyke, Raph Wallheimer and Mike Schmidt on the committee side, Andrew Grochal on architecture, embedded designers Jonathan Valdez (SRP), Alyssa Lesbirel (Discover), and Ting Liang (VDP). Later: Consumer AI / DEC eng (Jin Jin Heipler, Chris Cunningham), PDA (Kiersten Paniati, Luke Nau, Amanda Fabrizio), SRP (Jenn Kappel, Ravenne Nasser).

5. Scope and constraints

Q1 deadline, with a deliberate rule to work inside existing UI so anything designed could actually ship that quarter. SRP and VDP as the "standard bearer" track, more conservative than Discover. Dealer sensitivity: agent recommending other dealers' listings from a VDP (Raph pushback, 1/22). Legal risk on hallucinated vehicle facts. Existing SRP FABs (save search) and sticky ads competing for the same corners. Later: a -2% lead guardrail, shared "Ask Guru" name with Discover, an SRP redesign in test, and mobile latency (only 32% of mobile VDP users waited for the first response, per CNAI-573). In V2: a UAI Lead Form Refresh MVT runs on top of UAI_ENTRY_POINT in a 2x2 (MVT page), and leadership is reframing Guru success around trust rather than lead volume (Andrew, 9/29).

7. Outcomes and lessons learned

My work: Q1 behavior decisions trace to the January flows: SRP filters apply directly to the page rather than Discover's CTA-to-new-SRP model (Chris, 1/8), SRP leads confirm the vehicle with a mini-card before the form (2/19), and conversation persists across SRP and VDP. All three are in the shipped platform config (Guru Core).

OPEN: confirm the 2/19 mini-card call was yours or group consensus. Notes say consensus.

Conversation components landed in Chassis (CHASS-533, closed 3/10).

Contextual entry points, the position I argued on 1/15, became the CNAI-392 hypothesis: the FAB has low discoverability and no contextual value. VDP pills, pricing CTA, and FAB removal shipped into the UAI_ENTRY_POINT MVT from CNAI-210 (CNAI-403 / 408 / 409, closed 9/8 to 9/22).

Mobile sheet: 0 of 20 preferred Full over Med (CNAI-215). Rollout completed (CNAI-579) and it ships inside the V2 MVT.

VDP entry points as shipped in V2 trace to CNAI-210: an "Ask Guru" section below Features with 6 suggested prompts (horizontal scroll on mobile), a deal-rating "Why is this a good deal?" entry, and the FAB removed unless a conversation is active (AN-11569). The sticky header button replaced the sticky footer Ting had proposed (eng call, 9/17).

The UAI/Discover "Ask Guru" collision I flagged in CNAI-210 is now PDA recommendation #5, citing the CNAI-210 Figma.

Initiative-wide: V1 launched with the FAB. Run 1 (Apr 9 to May 5): 0.26% mobile open, a baseline only due to a MongoDB issue. Run 2 (Jul 8 to Aug 9): 0.40% mobile, not recommended by PDA, though inside the -2% guardrail.

V2 (UAI_ENTRY_POINT, AN-11569) is live. Activated 10/7, test start 10/8 at 1%, ramped to 5% on 10/27 and 10% on 10/29 (launch post). Status: Experimenting. No readout yet, interim or final; PDA's analysis doc has no V2 section.

Success bar: VDP-to-lead and SRP-to-lead within -0.5% (V1 was -0.79% mobile VDP), engaged-user VDP-to-lead at least 6% and SRP-to-lead at least 4%, UAI gains outweighing Discover cannibalization, and positive sentiment. Entry point click rate is a secondary metric, so for the first time there will be a number on my entry points specifically.

8. Process detail

Step 1: Framing the pattern (Jan 8)

  • What: first Consumer AI Pod. Proposed a persistent entry point plus contextual "wayfinding" entries that start the conversation with page context, and one conversation that follows the shopper SRP to VDP. Positioned SRP/VDP as the conservative standard bearer and Discover as the place for riskier patterns.
  • Why: Ting's VDP FAB was a valid near-term pattern but the SRP already had a save-search FAB in the same corner, so it would double up. On filtering, I leaned toward returning the shopper to the SRP with filters applied, not Discover's launch-a-new-SRP CTA, because that's how SRP already works.
  • Result: apply-filters-on-page is how UAI SRP shipped (autoApply true).
  • What I learned: > OPEN
  • Artifacts: 1/8 notes

Step 2: Contextual vs consistent (Jan 15)

  • What: presented SRP and VDP entry concepts: filter-integrated, persistent top-right, third-panel at wide breakpoints, inline desktop, FAB, and secondary text-link or icon entries.
  • Why: a FAB is easy to bolt on, which is why third-party chat vendors use it, but it reads as a separate widget. A filter-level entry puts the agent where the job is and implies it can change results. Jonathan made the same point: entry placement sets expectations about functionality.
  • Result: I leaned contextual; Sydney and Caitlin leaned FAB; Q1 shipped FAB. On mobile I accepted full-screen takeover as the main Q1 pattern, with half sheet noted as a later option.
  • What I learned: > OPEN. Candidate: I conceded both the FAB and the mobile takeover for Q1 feasibility, and both became the two Q3 fixes. What would you have pushed harder on?
  • Artifacts: 1/15 notes

    OPEN: Figma link for the January entry points file.

Step 3: Dialogue flows and behavior principles (Jan 9 to 22)

  • What: simulated multi-turn dialogues (up to 7 turns, ending in a dealer lead) with user messages, agent responses, tool calls, and generated components; behavior principles like progressive narrowing and "don't hallucinate"; a Claude skill that generates persona-based dialogues for voice, tone, and hallucination review.
  • Why: conversational flows branch, so static Figma frames couldn't answer engineering's question of what the agent actually does. The pitch was to define the MVP and let it be descoped, not wait for scope.
  • Result: shared with Raph; surfaced the dealer inventory question (recommending other dealers' cars from a VDP). Andrew wanted to know capabilities to avoid architecting into a corner.
  • What I learned: > OPEN. Your own 1/15 line is a candidate: most of the work was getting alignment on what to deliver, not the delivering.
  • Artifacts: dialogs doc · 1/22 notes

    OPEN: how the dealer inventory question resolved.

Step 4: Working agent prototype on Mastra (Jan 21 to Feb 13)

  • What: a working conversational agent built on Mastra, the TypeScript agent framework in eng's proposed UAI architecture. Contextually aware of the page, pulling real listing data ("cars good in the snow" returned real red and black listings). Presented live at the 1/22 Consumer AI Pod.
  • Why: conversation is interaction-heavy and branching, which Figma couldn't show. With Mastra the system prompt and behavior could be edited, then the same conversation replayed to see how the agent reacted, so behavior could be designed the way layout gets designed.
  • Result: presented 1/22 alongside the dialogue flows and shared with Raph; next step was sharing it with the pod to play with. Repo created 1/21, last modified 2/13, so roughly three weeks of active work. UAI later shipped on Mastra (universal-ai repo, Mastra Studio).
  • What I learned: > OPEN. Candidate from 1/22: prompt and behavior are design material, and you can only evaluate them by running them.
  • Artifacts: local repo mastra-agent (Chris's machine; contents not readable from here, macOS permissions blocked the directory listing) · 1/22 notes · architecture diagrams · glossary

Step 5: Shared conversation components (Jan to Mar)

  • What: inventory of conversation components (chat input, suggested prompts, listing carousel, FAB variants, actions bar), consolidated into the consumer library then Chassis.
  • Why: each team was building its own chat input and prompt style. Also argued for fewer sparkles inside the bot since the context already says AI.
  • Result: CHASS-533 closed 3/10. Suggested-prompt styling (plain vs Discover's perplexity-style) flagged for standardization.
  • What I learned: > OPEN
  • Artifacts: CHASS-533 · components doc

Step 6: Reopening entry points (CNAI-210, Apr 30 to Jul 30)

  • What: revisited placement with V1 data. Moved the bubble concept off bottom-right, designed primary persistent and secondary contextual entries on SRP and VDP, and co-pilot copy over support-chat copy.
  • Why: the VDP 31.8% vs SRP 7.3% engagement gap tracked with whether the welcome referenced the car in front of the shopper. Context was the lever.
  • Result: VDP question group became the "Ask Guru" pills and pricing CTA; SRP went to Jenn (RR-1499). Flagged the UAI/Discover naming collision.
  • What I learned: > OPEN
  • Artifacts: SRP concepts · VDP concepts · dev handoff

Step 7: Mobile sheet (CNAI-215 into CNAI-445, May 4 to Sep)

  • What: draggable Min / Med / Full sheet so chat and page coexist; live Chassis prototypes; 20-person test; spec for eng (input only at Full, half by default, "Ask anything" chip jumps to Full, scroll minimizes).
  • Why: full takeover hid the result of every filter action on SRP and the listing on VDP.
  • Result: 0 of 20 preferred Full. CNAI-452 and CNAI-508 closed; rolling out without an A/B test. Eng edge cases through 9/18.
  • Decision, 9/16: eng asked how entry points should open the sheet. Ting argued for Full. I'd specced Half and initially deferred, then held the line after Jin pointed out that opening everything at Full recreates the original takeover problem: entry pills other than "Ask anything" open at Half, so we'd see real behavior on the pattern. Also confirmed the in-chat "Ask anything" pill stays visually quieter than on-page entry pills, intentionally (thread).
  • What I learned: > OPEN. Candidate from 9/16: almost conceding the default state is the same move as conceding full takeover in January. Scroll-to-collapse also went against the Google Maps hold-mode pattern; did it hold up?
  • Artifacts: CNAI-215 Figma · prototype · MVT page

    OPEN: full test readout deck.

Step 8: V2 live with the new entry points (Oct 7 to now)

  • What: UAI_ENTRY_POINT MVT launched with the CNAI-210 VDP entry points, mobile SRP CTA next to filters, and the CNAI-215 sheet. Ramping 1% to 5% to 10% over three weeks.
  • Why: V1 failed on reach; V2's hypothesis is that contextual entry plus a better mobile experience raises engagement and lead conversion. That's the bet from 1/15, now in test.
  • Result: > PENDING AN-11569. Note desktop SRP still shows the FAB (SRP entry points are mobile only), so desktop SRP is effectively a V1-style control for entry. Desktop FAB tracking source was fixed after launch (CNAI-625).
  • What I learned: > OPEN, after readout.
  • Related signal: John Huang (research, 10/2) found that showing what Guru can do beats pitching it, and that a floating "Ask Guru" button doesn't convey value. That's independent support for the suggested-prompt entry points over the FAB (post). Andrew's 9/29 framing: separate entry engagement from quality and trust, so opens alone don't count as success.
